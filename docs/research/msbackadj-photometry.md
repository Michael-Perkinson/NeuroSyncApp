# Research: `msbackadj` and independent bleaching correction in fiber photometry

**Accessed:** 2026-09-22. **Scope:** literature, official documentation, and publication-linked MATLAB code; no application code was changed.

## Summary

`msbackadj` is a generic, peak-preserving baseline estimator, not a fiber-photometry-specific or 405/410-nm-dependent method. It estimates local intensity quantiles in windows, fits/interpolates those baseline anchors, and subtracts the resulting curve (with an option to preserve peak heights). It could therefore be applied independently to green and red traces using time as `X`, but the window, quantile, and subtraction model must be validated against the sensor kinetics and the timescale of biological signals.

Freely behaving-mouse precedents use substantially different scales: the Dai et al. Nature paper reports a window equal to 20% of the recording, Han and Morris et al. use 900 s for long-duration kisspeptin recordings, and a Dai/Guo/Lin multi-fiber protocol uses 10 s while explicitly warning that minute-scale transients can be removed. The associated deposited MATLAB archive is especially important: its repeated `msbackadj` calls set both `WindowSize` and `StepSize` to `floor(recording_duration/10)`, i.e. 10% rather than the paper's reported 20%. Because the archive README labels it `GRAB_DA_Fig6_Code` and calls it “related” MATLAB code, this is a reproducibility discrepancy/limitation, not definitive proof of the private figure-generation script and not evidence for a universal percentage. Independent per-channel correction is also supported when no suitable control exists (notably red-shifted indicators without a known isosbestic point), but it corrects slow baseline/fading and cannot by itself identify movement, hemodynamic, or spectral cross-talk artifacts.

## Findings

1. **Claim — what MathWorks says `msbackadj` does.** The official documentation describes correction of a slowly varying baseline in signals containing peaks. In its default procedure, windows are placed along `X` (`WindowSize` and `StepSize`); each window contributes a baseline estimate. The default `EstimationMethod` is `quantile`, with default `QuantileValue = 0.10`; the estimates can be smoothed and are fit to a continuous baseline using the default `RegressionMethod = 'pchip'` (with linear/spline alternatives). The corrected signal is obtained by subtracting the baseline; `PreserveHeights` can retain original peak heights. **Sources:** [MathWorks, `msbackadj`](https://www.mathworks.com/help/bioinfo/ref/msbackadj.html). **Support:** direct evidence from official documentation; the description that this is a sparse-window quantile-anchor fit rather than a pointwise running quantile is an interpretation of those documented steps. **Confidence:** high.

2. **Claim — relevance to photometry.** With `X = time`, `msbackadj` is mathematically usable as an independent slow-trend estimator on each channel, e.g., green and red separately. This is a researcher inference, not a claim that MathWorks validated it for photometry. Use windows longer than expected neural transients but short enough to follow bleaching; test sensitivity to `WindowSize`, `StepSize`, `QuantileValue`, and `PreserveHeights`. A low quantile can be biased upward when the trace is densely occupied by activity, while a short window can subtract slow biological responses. **Sources:** [MathWorks, `msbackadj`](https://www.mathworks.com/help/bioinfo/ref/msbackadj.html). **Support:** inference from the documented estimator and photometry timescale problem. **Confidence:** medium.

3. **Claim — a published fiber-photometry protocol uses a 10-second `msbackadj` window.** Dai, Guo, and Lin’s *STAR Protocols* method applies `msbackadj` when calculating dF/F to correct photobleaching. The authors explicitly warn that the correction can remove slow transients on the scale of minutes and recommend raw fluorescence when those dynamics are important. The protocol states the 10-second window but does not report a photometry-specific `QuantileValue` or `StepSize`. **Sources:** [Dai et al. 2024, *Brain-wide multi-fiber recording of neuronal activity in freely moving mice*, PMID 38340320, DOI 10.1016/j.xpro.2024.102882](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10873755/). **Support:** direct methods/protocol evidence. **Confidence:** high for the 10-second window; low for any unreported percentage or step size.

4. **Claim — green/red dual-color photometry in freely moving mice commonly requires spectral correction first.** Meng et al. simultaneously recorded green GCaMP6f and red jRGECO1a in striatal pathways of freely moving mice. Because emission spectra overlapped, they used frame-wise linear spectral unmixing with reference spectra; the unmixed coefficients represented individual fluorophore intensities. They observed jRGECO1a fading and corrected it by single- or double-decay nonlinear regression, then calculated `ΔCo/Co0%` and session z-scores. No 405/410 control was used for this fading correction. **Sources:** [Meng et al., “Spectrally Resolved Fiber Photometry for Multi-component Analysis of Brain Circuits,” Neuron (2018), PMID 29731250, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2018.04.012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5957785/). **Support:** direct methods evidence. **Confidence:** high.

5. **Claim — a dual-color protocol uses a moving baseline independently, without a 405/410 control.** Serikov et al. recorded GCaMP6m (green) and jRCaMP1a (red) and calculated each channel’s `ΔF/F` using a one-minute sliding moving average baseline. The protocol explicitly says this was intended to correct slow photobleaching and did not use calcium-independent signals because a red-GECI isosbestic point is generally unknown and the environment was considered stable. It cautions that the method suits short events but may miss or distort changes occurring slowly over long periods. **Sources:** [Serikov et al., “Protocol for in vivo dual-color fiber photometry in the mouse thalamus,” STAR Protocols (2024), PMID 38470909, DOI 10.1016/j.xpro.2024.102931](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10943959/). **Support:** direct methods evidence. **Confidence:** high.

6. **Claim — independent channel bleaching correction is explicitly recommended in a freely moving-animal protocol.** Martianova et al. use the airPLS algorithm to remove exponential decreases separately from each channel, then align the 410-nm control to the 470-nm signal for movement/artifact correction. This demonstrates that “independent bleaching correction” and “control-channel artifact correction” are separable operations; it does *not* support treating the corrected signal as independent of a control for motion artifacts. **Sources:** [Martianova et al., “Multi-Fiber Photometry to Record Neural Activity in Freely-Moving Animals,” JoVE (2019), PMID 31680685, DOI 10.3791/60278](https://www.jove.com/t/60278/multi-fiber-photometry-to-record-neural-activity-freely-moving). **Support:** direct methods evidence. **Confidence:** high.

7. **Claim — software pipelines offer no-control alternatives, but not `msbackadj`.** GuPPy applies a zero-phase moving-average filter and, when no isosbestic channel is supplied, generates a control by smoothing and fitting an exponential to approximate bleaching; it then fits that control to the signal with a degree-1 least-squares polynomial and computes `ΔF/F`. Thus an independent correction route exists in practice, but it is a smoothing/exponential-plus-regression pipeline, not `msbackadj`. **Sources:** [Sherathiya et al., “GuPPy,” Scientific Reports (2021), PMID 34930955, PMCID PMC8688475, DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-03626-9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8688475/); [GuPPy repository](https://github.com/LernerLab/GuPPy). **Support:** direct methods evidence. **Confidence:** high.

8. **Claim — published alternatives include polynomial/low-pass, airPLS, and decay fits.** A mouse sleep protocol fits 405 nm to 465 nm with a first-degree polynomial, subtracts and divides to obtain `dF/F`; the dual-color spectrally resolved protocol fits linear or one-/two-phase decay curves to fading coefficients; and the multi-fiber protocol uses airPLS per channel before control alignment. These are not interchangeable: subtraction assumes bleaching mainly changes baseline, whereas division assumes it also scales physiological amplitude. **Sources:** [Using Fiber Photometry in Mice to Estimate Fluorescent Biosensor Levels During Sleep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10415158/); [Spectrally resolved dual-color protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10018997/); [Martianova et al.](https://www.jove.com/t/60278/multi-fiber-photometry-to-record-neural-activity-freely-moving). **Support:** direct evidence plus interpretation of subtraction/division assumptions. **Confidence:** high.

9. **Claim — control regression and independent bleaching correction answer different artifact questions.** Patel et al.’s primer states that bleaching should be estimated by slow filtering or curve fitting and corrected by subtraction or division; it cautions that isosbestic and signal channels can have different autofluorescence/indicator contributions and therefore different bleaching kinetics. It also describes the common control workflow (fit control to signal, subtract fitted control, divide by fitted control) and its implicit assumption that relative movement and bleaching effects match. **Sources:** [Patel et al., “Lights, fiber, action! A primer on in vivo fiber photometry,” 2024, PMCID PMC10939905](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10939905/). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

10. **Claim — robust regression can reduce activity being absorbed into the control fit.** A recent simulation/real-data study found IRLS with Tukey’s bisquare outperformed OLS for fitting isosbestic controls, and that dF/F outperformed dF during simulated event periods. This study concerns 405/465 control fitting (and static fluorophore controls conceptually), not proof that independent `msbackadj` correction is superior. **Sources:** [Obtaining artifact-corrected signals in fiber photometry via isosbestic signals, robust regression, and dF/F calculations, PMCID PMC11957252](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11957252/). **Support:** direct evidence; application to `msbackadj` is not established. **Confidence:** high.

11. **Claim — red sensors may lack a useful isosbestic point.** The thalamus protocol’s explicit rationale is that the red-GECI isosbestic point is generally unknown. More broadly, a red sensor without an isosbestic control can be paired with a spectrally compatible static reference fluorophore, but that reference must be demonstrated to be activity-independent and spectrally separated. **Sources:** [Serikov et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xpro.2024.102931); [Improved green and red GRAB sensors, DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02188-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02188-8). **Support:** direct evidence for red-GECI limitation; recommendation is source-supported interpretation. **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Newly verified freely behaving-mouse evidence and publication code

 1. **Claim — the target Dai et al. Nature paper reports a 20% window.** In *A dedicated hypothalamic oxytocin circuit controls aversive social learning* (Nature 2024), the freely moving mouse methods say that MATLAB `msbackadj` was first applied with a moving window equal to **20% of the total recording duration** to obtain the instantaneous baseline. They then calculate instantaneous ΔF/F as `(Fraw − Fbaseline) / Fbaseline` and z-score the entire session. **Sources:** [Nature article, PMCID PMC11102773](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11102773/); [DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06958-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06958-w). **Support:** direct methods evidence. **Confidence:** high for what the paper reports.

 2. **Claim — the relevant deposited scripts use 10%, not 20%.** The associated [Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.8417540](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8417540) identifies raw data and MATLAB code for the Nature paper. Its `related matlab code.zip` expands to a folder whose README is titled `GRAB_DA_Fig6_Code`; inspection found `msbackadj` in `FPanalysis_overall.m`, `PSTH_all_raw.m`, and `timeShiftCorrlation.m`. Each script contains the same calculation, `W = floor(length(Lfilter)/FL/10)`, and passes `W` as both `StepSize` and `windowsize`; no corresponding 20% setting appears in those scripts. `ExtractData.m` first low-pass filters each raw channel with a fourth-order, zero-phase 3-Hz Butterworth filter. **Support:** direct inspection of the deposited archive. **Confidence:** high for the inspected scripts; because the archive is labeled “related” code, it cannot prove which code version generated every published panel.

 3. **Claim — the deposited Nature workflow is independent per channel and divisive after baseline estimation.** In `FPanalysis_overall.m`, `Lfilter` and `Lfilter2` are processed independently. The `msbackadj` output is called `Lflat`; the estimated baseline is reconstructed as `Lbackground = Lfilter - Lflat`; and the final signal is `Lfilter = Lflat ./ Lbackground`. The first 10 seconds are left outside the `msbackadj` call (`Lflat` is initialized to zero there), so that stabilization interval should not be interpreted as a valid processed trace. This is a useful concrete precedent, but the 10%-versus-20% discrepancy—and the archive's “related GRAB_DA” labeling—must be reported rather than silently resolved.

 4. **Claim — a separate freely behaving-mouse codebase uses 900 s.** The publicly released [`feszilvi/Fiber_photom`](https://github.com/feszilvi/Fiber_photom) script, associated with Han and Morris et al.'s 2023 Cell Reports kisspeptin study, subtracts 405 nm from 490 nm, calls `msbackadj(TSxDS, TS490405DS, 'WindowSize', BSTimeWindow, 'StepSize', BSTimeWindow)` with `BSTimeWindow = 900`, then z-scores the corrected signal. The paper's methods independently report a 900-s window for freely behaving photometry and peak detection on the z-scored trace. The code also applies a second 600-s `msbackadj` call to the z-scored signal for peak-oriented baseline fitting. **Sources:** [Han et al. 2023, PMCID PMC7618808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618808/); [GitHub code](https://github.com/feszilvi/Fiber_photom); [Zenodo software release 10.5281/zenodo.15320261](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15320261). **Support:** direct paper/code evidence. **Confidence:** high.

 5. **Claim — the 10-s protocol precedent is reproducible from a public archive.** Dai, Guo, and Lin's 2024 *STAR Protocols* paper for multi-fiber recording in freely moving mice explicitly says that dF/F stored in `Lfilter` uses `msbackadj` with a **10-second window** for photobleaching, and warns that minute-scale slow transients may be removed. It deposits MFP extraction code and an example dataset at [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.8428784](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8428784) (latest record: [10.5281/zenodo.10396062](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10396062)). This is direct evidence for a short-window photobleaching use case, not evidence that 10 s is suitable for long-duration biological changes. **Sources:** [Dai et al. 2024, PMCID PMC10873755](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10873755/); [Zenodo code/data record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10396062). **Confidence:** high.

 6. **Claim — another freely moving-mouse paper reports 10% but its public example code does not contain `msbackadj`.** Chen et al.'s multi-fiber social-behavior paper describes `msbackadj` with a moving window of 10% of the recording duration, then defines the instantaneous baseline as `Fraw − Fflat`. Its [public repository](https://github.com/YiboChen2020/MFPBehavioralAnalysis) provides HSMM/PSID example analysis, but a recursive search of the checked-out MATLAB/Python files found no `msbackadj` call; the README says code for some processing methods is available only on request. Therefore this is a methods-only 10% precedent, unlike the directly inspected Nature and Fiber_photom scripts. **Sources:** [Chen et al., PMCID PMC11246699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11246699/); [repository](https://github.com/YiboChen2020/MFPBehavioralAnalysis). **Confidence:** high for the paper statement; limited for reproducibility from the public example repository.

## Benefits, failure modes, and cautions for independent correction

- **Benefits:** no extra 405/410 acquisition; works per fluorophore despite different bleaching/fading kinetics; preserves green/red channel identity; useful when a red sensor has no known isosbestic point; can make a slow baseline visible and normalize short phasic events.
- **Failure mode—biological low-frequency signal removal:** any windowed quantile, moving average, high-pass filter, or flexible spline can absorb genuine slow neuromodulator/calcium responses. Validate with synthetic transients and event durations, and inspect the baseline against behavior.
- **Failure mode—baseline contamination:** if activity occupies much of a window, the 10th percentile (or any selected quantile) may not represent an unmodulated baseline. Conversely, too-low a quantile can follow noise/floor effects.
- **Failure mode—wrong amplitude model:** `msbackadj`’s standard output is subtractive. If indicator bleaching scales transient amplitude, division by an estimated baseline may be more defensible; if autofluorescence dominates, subtraction may be preferable. The literature does not establish one universal choice.
- **Failure mode—non-bleaching artifacts:** independent detrending cannot identify motion, fiber bending, hemodynamic absorption, LED power changes, detector saturation, or green-to-red bleed-through. Meng et al. show that spectral overlap can create an apparent red response, requiring spectral unmixing rather than baseline correction.
- **Caution—do not infer control independence:** absence of a 405/410 channel supports only “no isosbestic control was acquired.” It does not prove the trace is free of motion/hemodynamic artifacts. A static red fluorophore is a control only when its activity-independence and spectral behavior are established.
- **Caution—order of operations:** perform spectral unmixing/background subtraction before per-channel fading correction where relevant; retain raw data; report method, window/step, quantile, interpolation, filter phase/cutoff, fit domain, subtraction/division, and excluded stabilization period.

## Evidence-weighted recommendation for freely behaving mouse photometry

**NeuroSync implementation note:** DFer option 5 applies the 10% `msbackadj` correction independently to each selected signal and intentionally does not use the 405/410 control in the selected analysis path. The selected option is stored once in the same CSV header as `# option used=5`; the extra column's data cells are blank, so it is not repeated as a data value or placed on a separate metadata row. The single-channel `dFoF_405`/`Z_405` outputs are empty for option 5 and the results view hides that control trace. In dual output, the legacy fitted-control column positions contain each target's independent fitted baseline. This is a baseline/bleaching choice, not motion or hemodynamic correction.

The best-supported use case is **slow baseline/photobleaching correction before quantifying phasic events that are clearly shorter than the chosen baseline window**, especially after a control/background subtraction or when no trustworthy isosbestic/reference channel is available. The evidence does *not* justify a universal `msbackadj` window:

- Use the **paper-reported 20%** only when reproducing the Dai et al. Nature method, and label it as a reported method rather than a validated general default. The associated deposited scripts currently inspected use **10%**, so a reproduction should preserve both values as an explicit paper/code discrepancy and compare them.
- The **10-s** setting is a short-event/photobleaching choice. The authors explicitly warn that it can remove minute-scale signals; it is inappropriate by default when slow behavioral or neuromodulator dynamics matter.
- The **900-s** setting is a long-recording precedent for kisspeptin synchronization-event detection, with an additional 600-s fit in the released script. It is not interchangeable with the 10-s protocol or the 20% report.

For a new dataset, select `WindowSize` in **seconds** from the slowest biological change that must be preserved and the fastest bleaching/drift that must be followed. Set `StepSize` explicitly, initially equal to `WindowSize` only if that sampling density is justified, and run a sensitivity analysis across plausible longer windows. Record the actual window, step, omitted stabilization interval, MATLAB defaults (including the default 10% quantile unless overridden), and whether correction is subtractive or divisive. Always retain and inspect the raw trace, the estimated baseline, and a control/movement correlation; `msbackadj` alone does not correct motion, hemodynamic absorption, LED/detector changes, or spectral cross-talk.

## Contradictions

The literature does not provide a consensus `msbackadj` percentage or step size for fiber photometry. Qualifying freely behaving-mouse examples span 10 s, 900 s, 10% of the recording, 20% of the recording, and a target-paper code path using 10% where the paper reports 20%. These settings correspond to different recording lengths and biological questions, so they should not be pooled as if they were a single recommended parameter. The literature also disagrees on subtraction versus division for bleaching, reflecting different assumptions about whether indicator fluorescence contributes to amplitude scaling ([Patel et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10939905/)).

## Missing evidence

- The target Nature paper's prose reports 20%, while the associated “related” archive's inspected scripts use 10%; the deposited files do not establish whether another private or figure-specific script used 20%.
- No qualifying paper located here reports a photometry-specific `QuantileValue`; the 10% quantile remains the MathWorks default, not a validated photometry choice.
- No systematic head-to-head benchmark was found comparing `msbackadj` with moving quantile/median, airPLS, exponential, or low-pass correction on matched freely behaving mouse datasets.
- The correct window/quantile for a particular sensor, behavior, and sampling rate remains empirical; the 10-s, 900-s, 10%, and 20% precedents should all be treated as hypotheses to validate, not defaults.

## Dataset-specific 10% versus 900-s comparison

A preliminary comparison was run on the available CFD4 recording (about 70.2 min; 10% = 421.2 s) using the Python port in `.scratch/msbackadj-sandbox/`. MATLAB is unavailable, so these are diagnostic port results, not proof of exact MATLAB equivalence. Both methods used the same documented defaults (`QuantileValue = 0.10`, `RegressionMethod = 'pchip'`, no height preservation) and `StepSize = WindowSize`, matching the two deposited code paths.

| Signal | Baseline correlation | 10% std dF/F | 900-s std dF/F | 10% retention* | 900-s retention* |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| CH1-470 | 0.982 | 1.94% | 3.06% | 0.61 | 0.40 |
| CH1-560 | 0.997 | 8.91% | 9.67% | 0.84 | 0.79 |

\*Retention is correlation with a high-pass reference of the same raw trace; it is a diagnostic, not a validated measure of biological truth. The baseline curves are highly correlated overall, but the 470-nm plot shows a meaningful difference at the late-session upward drift: the 421-s window follows it more closely, while the 900-s window leaves more of it in the corrected signal. If that rise is bleaching, 10% is preferable; if it is a real biological change, 10% may remove it. The comparison therefore supports testing both against behavior/control channels rather than choosing 900 s solely because it is fixed. The 410-nm control supports interpreting the late rise as shared artifact/drift: over minutes 60–70, smoothed raw slopes were approximately **+1.66%/min for 410** and **+1.48%/min for 470**; their 10% baseline changes had slope correlation **r = 0.999**. This makes the 10% correction more defensible for this recording, although it does not identify whether the shared component is bleaching, movement, or another optical artifact.

## Next steps

If implementation is later considered, benchmark per-channel/control-subtracted `msbackadj` against moving median/quantile, airPLS, and single/double exponential fits on representative raw green/red traces with injected slow biological components. Include the paper/code discrepancy as two explicit reproduction conditions (10% and 20%), plus the 10-s and 900-s published precedents, and pre-register acceptance criteria based on preserved event amplitude, slow-signal retention, and residual movement correlation.
